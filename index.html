About the physics

Journeys use the exact relativistic rocket equation (rapidity = exhaust speed × ln mass ratio) with constant thrust, integrated in the ship's own frame. "Earth time" is the time that passes at home; "ship time" is what the crew's clocks record. Beyond 10 million light-years the ship is flown through an expanding ΛCDM universe (Planck 2018: H₀ = 67.7 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.31, ΩΛ = 0.69): coasting ships slow relative to the local Hubble flow, and nothing can reach places beyond the cosmic event horizon. Map distances are present-day (comoving) distances; we see distant objects as they were when their light set out. Planets are fixed at their positions on 1 January 2027; trips to them fly a straight line from Earth and ignore the Sun’s gravity, so a minimum-energy (Hohmann) transfer is shown alongside for comparison. Nearby star positions are real; the Milky Way's structure, the galaxy field and the cosmic web are illustrative.
